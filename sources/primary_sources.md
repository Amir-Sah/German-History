# Primary Sources — Reading Guide

*Primary sources used or recommended, by period, with what they can and cannot tell you. Most are available (bilingual) in the German Historical Institute's **German History in Documents and Images (GHDI)**, https://germanhistorydocs.org/ — ten volumes, 1500–2023, project 2003–2026.*

| Period | Source | What it shows | Source-critical caution |
|---|---|---|---|
| Antiquity | Caesar, *De Bello Gallico* | Roman categorisation of *Germani* | Political self-justification (why Caesar stopped at the Rhine) |
| Antiquity | Tacitus, *Germania* (c. 98) | Roman ethnographic image | Moral mirror for Rome; second-hand information; later nationalist misuse |
| Carolingian | *Admonitio generalis* (789); *Capitulatio de partibus Saxoniae* (c. 782/85) | Royal programmes; forced Christianisation | Prescriptive, not descriptive |
| Carolingian | Royal Frankish Annals | Court narrative (e.g., Verden 782) | Court propaganda; numbers doubtful |
| Medieval | Golden Bull (1356) | Electoral procedure and princely privileges | Constitutional text, not a description of practice |
| Medieval | Town charters; *Weistümer* | Local law and custom | Often written down late; reflect negotiated ideals |
| Reformation | Luther's writings (1517–1543) | Theology, politics, anti-Judaism | Polemical; changing positions |
| Reformation | Twelve Articles (1525) | Peasant demands in scriptural language | Programme of a movement, not every peasant's view |
| Reformation | Peace of Augsburg (1555) | Confessional settlement | Ambiguous clauses deliberately left open |
| 30 Years' War | Peace of Westphalia (1648) | Imperial constitution, religious parity | Read the text, not later IR myths (Osiander) |
| 30 Years' War | Hans Heberle's *Zeytregister*; Grimmelshausen's *Simplicissimus* (1668) | Lived experience | Individual/literary perspectives |
| Enlightenment | Kant, "What is Enlightenment?" (1784) | Public reason under enlightened monarchy | Elite perspective |
| Prussia | Allgemeines Landrecht (1794) | Estate society codified | Law vs practice |
| Napoleonic | October Edict (1807); Regulation Edict (1811) | Peasant emancipation terms | Implementation outcomes differ (land cessions) |
| Vormärz | Carlsbad Decrees (1819); Hambach speeches (1832); Büchner, *Der Hessische Landbote* (1834) | Repression vs opposition | Speeches are advocacy |
| 1848 | Basic Rights (Dec 1848); Paulskirche Constitution (Mar 1849) | Liberal-democratic programme | Never implemented |
| Unification | Bismarck's "iron and blood" speech (1862); Ems dispatch (1870); Reich Constitution (1871) | Power politics; constitutional design | Constitution hides Prussian hegemony and army autonomy |
| Kaiserreich | Erfurt Programme (1891); Bernstein; Reichstag debates; social insurance laws | Labour movement; state responses | Party programmes vs practice |
| Colonial | Trotha's orders (1904–05); Reichstag debates | Genocidal intent; domestic critique | Colonial archives from perpetrator side |

## How to read primary sources critically (checklist used in this project)

1. **Who wrote it, for whom, and why?** (Propaganda? Petition? Private diary? Legal text?)
2. **Prescriptive or descriptive?** Laws, programmes and decrees tell you what someone *wanted*, not what *happened*.
3. **Whose voice is missing?** (Peasants, women, Jews, colonised people, workers often appear only through others' records.)
4. **When was it written relative to events?** (Memoirs after 1945 routinely exculpate — e.g., generals' and Speer's memoirs.)
5. **Is it corroborated by independent sources?**
