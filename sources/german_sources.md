# German-Language and German Institutional Sources — Assessment

| Institution | What it offers | Strengths | Limitations / orientation | Used for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Bundeszentrale für politische Bildung (bpb)** — *Informationen zur politischen Bildung* (IzpB), *Aus Politik und Zeitgeschichte* (APuZ), dossiers | Signed overviews by leading historians (Gotthard, Osterhammel, Ziemann, Piper, Hoffmann, Böick, Rother, Pagenstecher) | Scholarly, up to date, bibliographies, free | Mandate: democratic civic education — tends toward "democratic learning" narratives; relatively cautious on political controversies | Backbone for 1500–present institutional narrative |
| **Deutsches Historisches Museum / Haus der Geschichte — LeMO** | Short encyclopedic chapters, objects, timelines | Institutional reliability; broad coverage; authored texts | Brief; some celebratory framing (e.g., resistance chapter's claim that Germany "broke with dictatorship through its own strength" is contestable) | Facts, figures, orientation |
| **Bundesarchiv** (incl. Stasi-Unterlagen-Archiv MfS-Lexikon; Weimar virtual exhibitions) | Archival data and definitions | Primary data (e.g., 91,015 Stasi staff) | Descriptive, definitional | Hard figures |
| **Deutscher Bundestag** (text archive, research service WD, exhibitions) | Parliamentary history; legal texts; research papers | Authoritative on parliamentary facts | Institutional self-presentation | Enabling Act, Two-plus-Four, recent laws, denazification |
| **German Historical Institute Washington — GHDI** | Bilingual primary documents with scholarly introductions | Primary sources + expert editing | Selection shapes narrative | Primary sources throughout |
| **Historisches Lexikon Bayerns** | Signed encyclopedia articles | Scholarly; regional depth | Bavarian focus | End of the Reich (Burgdorf); manorialism |

## German historiographical traditions to be aware of

- **Borussian school (19th c.):** Prussia's "mission" — legitimised kleindeutsch unification; source of the "failed Empire" narrative.
- **Post-1945 West German conservative historiography (Ritter, Rothfels):** Nazism as aberration; resistance celebrated.
- **Bielefeld school (Wehler, Kocka):** Social history; Sonderweg.
- **GDR Marxist historiography:** Class struggle; "early bourgeois revolution" (1525); antifascist legitimation; valuable empirical work but ideological framing.
- **Everyday-history (*Alltagsgeschichte*) (Lüdtke, Broszat's Bavaria project):** Experience and *Eigen-Sinn*.
- **Cultural/ritual turn (Stollberg-Rilinger, Althoff).**
- **Post-1990 reunified historiography:** GDR research boom; global/colonial turn.
