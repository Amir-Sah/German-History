## A. Before any "German" polity (to c.500)

| Anchor | Event | What changed |
|---|---|---|
| 1st c. BCE | Caesar names peoples east of the Rhine *Germani* | A Roman label is born; it later becomes an identity myth |
| 9 CE | Varus's three legions destroyed (probably near Kalkriese) | Rome abandons plans for a province up to the Elbe; Rhine–Danube frontier stabilises |
| c.98 CE | Tacitus, *Germania* | Roman moral ethnography; rediscovered 15th c. and used by nationalists |
| c.120–200 | Upper German–Raetian *Limes* built out | A monitored, trading border, not a sealed wall |
| c.260 | *Limes* abandoned | Alamanni settle the Agri Decumates (SW Germany) |
| 4th–5th c. | Frankish, Alamannic, Saxon, Thuringian, Bavarian groupings; Western Empire dissolves (476) | Roman provincial cities (Cologne, Trier, Mainz, Augsburg, Regensburg) survive as church centres |

## B. Frankish world (c.500–911)

| Anchor | Event | What changed |
|---|---|---|
| c.496–508 | Clovis's baptism | Frankish kingship allied with Latin Christianity |
| 8th c. | Boniface organises church east of the Rhine | Bishoprics = first territorial administration in much of Germany |
| 772–804 | Charlemagne's Saxon Wars | Saxony forcibly Christianised and incorporated |
| 800 | Charlemagne crowned emperor in Rome | Western imperial idea revived — links "empire" to the pope and Rome |
| 843 | Treaty of Verdun | Carolingian realm divided; East Francia = seed of the later German kingdom |
| 911 | Last East Frankish Carolingian dies; Conrad I elected | Kingship by election of dukes, not only by descent |

## C. The medieval Holy Roman Empire (919–c.1500)

| Anchor | Event | What changed |
|---|---|---|
| 919 | Henry I (Saxon/Ottonian) king | Kingship moves to Saxony; kingdom survives as a federation of duchies |
| 955 | Lechfeld: Otto I defeats Magyars | Royal prestige; eastern frontier secured |
| 962 | Otto I crowned emperor | German kingship fused with Roman emperorship — the "Reich" begins |
| 1075–1122 | Investiture Controversy (Canossa 1077; Concordat of Worms 1122) | Kings lose sacral control of bishops; princes gain leverage |
| 12th–14th c. | *Ostsiedlung*: settlement east of the Elbe; town foundations; Hanse | New lordships, towns and mixed German-Slavic societies in the east |
| 1096; 1348–50 | Rhineland massacres; Black Death pogroms | Jewish communities destroyed repeatedly |
| 1250s–1273 | "Interregnum" | Princes consolidate; weak kingship becomes normal |
| 1356 | Golden Bull | Seven electors fixed; election by majority; electors' lands indivisible |
| 1438 onward | Habsburgs almost continuously emperors | Empire tied to a dynasty whose power base is outside "Germany" |
| 1495 | Diet of Worms: Perpetual Public Peace; Imperial Chamber Court | "Imperial Reform" — law and courts replace feud |

## D. Early modern Reich: reformations, war, territorial states (1500–1806)

| Anchor | Event | What changed |
|---|---|---|
| 1517 | Luther's theses | Reform movement; printing makes it mass-media event |
| 1524–26 | Peasants' War (Twelve Articles, 1525) | ~70–100k killed; commoners' political voice suppressed, but princes also learn to negotiate |
| 1526/1555 | Speyer; Peace of Augsburg | Princes decide their territory's confession (*cuius regio, eius religio*, later formula); churches become state institutions |
| 1618–1648 | Thirty Years' War | Catastrophic mortality (≈20–40% regionally varied); Westphalia re-constitutes the Empire |
| 1648 | Peace of Westphalia | Estates' *Landeshoheit* confirmed within imperial law; religious parity; "normal year" 1624 |
| 1663–1806 | "Perpetual Diet" at Regensburg | Empire as permanent congress of princes |
| 1683–1699 | Habsburg victories over Ottomans | Austria becomes a great power, largely outside the Empire |
| 1701 | Brandenburg elector crowned "King in Prussia" | Hohenzollern royal title (for lands outside the Empire) |
| 1740–1763 | Silesian Wars / Seven Years' War | Prussia seizes Silesia; Austro-Prussian dualism |
| 1772–1795 | Partitions of Poland | Prussia and Austria gain large Polish populations |
| 1784 | Kant, "What is Enlightenment?" | Enlightenment as public reasoning under a reforming monarch |
| 1794 | Prussian General Law Code (ALR) | Codified law within a society of estates |

## E. Napoleonic upheaval and the German Confederation (1792–1866)

| Anchor | Event | What changed |
|---|---|---|
| 1801–1803 | Lunéville; *Reichsdeputationshauptschluss* | Church states and ~45 imperial cities absorbed; ~5 million people get new rulers; middle states (Bavaria, Württemberg, Baden) enlarged |
| 1806 | Confederation of the Rhine; Francis II lays down crown | Holy Roman Empire ends |
| 1806–1807 | Prussia defeated (Jena–Auerstedt; Tilsit) | Reform era: October Edict 1807, municipal self-government, military reform |
| 1813–1815 | "Wars of Liberation"; Congress of Vienna | German Confederation (39, later ~41 members); Prussia gains Rhineland & Westphalia |
| 1819 | Carlsbad Decrees | Censorship, university surveillance |
| 1832 | Hambach Festival | Liberal-national mass rally |
| 1834 | *Zollverein* (customs union) | Economic integration under Prussian leadership, excluding Austria |
| 1835 | First German railway (Nuremberg–Fürth) | Start of railway-led industrialisation |
| 1844–47 | Silesian weavers' revolt; hunger crisis | *Pauperismus* peaks; emigration surges |
| 1848–49 | Revolutions; Frankfurt Parliament; Paulskirche constitution (Mar 1849) | Basic rights, national parliament — defeated militarily by 1849 |
| 1850s | Reaction; Prussian constitution (1850) with three-class suffrage | Constitutional monarchy with weighted vote |
| 1862–66 | Prussian constitutional conflict; Bismarck; wars of 1864 & 1866 | Austria excluded; North German Confederation 1867 |

## F. The German Empire (1871–1918)

| Anchor | Event | What changed |
|---|---|---|
| 18 Jan 1871 | Empire proclaimed at Versailles | Federal monarchy under Prussia; Alsace-Lorraine annexed |
| 1871–1887 | *Kulturkampf* | State vs Catholic Church; Catholic milieu consolidated (Centre Party) |
| 1873 | "Founders' crash" | Long depression; turn to protectionism (1879); rise of organised antisemitism |
| 1878–1890 | Anti-Socialist Law | SPD persecuted yet grows |
| 1883–1889 | Health, accident, old-age insurance | Early social-insurance state |
| 1884–1885 | Colonies acquired; Berlin Conference | Colonial empire in Africa & Pacific |
| 1890 | Bismarck dismissed; "New Course" | Wilhelmine era; Weltpolitik, naval race |
| 1904–1908 | Herero and Nama genocide | Recognised by Germany as genocide in 2021 |
| 1912 | SPD largest party in Reichstag (~35%) | Mass democratic politics inside semi-authoritarian frame |
| 1914 | War; *Burgfrieden* | Political truce; SPD votes war credits |
| 1916–1918 | 3rd Supreme Army Command (Hindenburg/Ludendorff) | Military effectively dominates politics; hunger at home |
| Oct–Nov 1918 | Parliamentarisation; sailors' revolt; Kaiser abdicates; armistice | Monarchy collapses |

## G. The Weimar Republic (1918/19–1933)

| Anchor | Event | What changed |
|---|---|---|
| 12 Nov 1918 | Council of People's Deputies announces women's suffrage | Universal suffrage from age 20 |
| Jan 1919 | Spartacist uprising crushed; National Assembly elected | SPD–Freikorps alliance; lasting left split |
| Jun–Aug 1919 | Versailles Treaty; Weimar Constitution | Parliamentary republic with strong president (Art. 48) |
| 1920 | Kapp Putsch defeated by general strike | Republic survives, army ambivalent |
| 1923 | Ruhr occupation; hyperinflation; Hitler putsch | Middle-class savings wiped out |
| 1924–1929 | Dawes Plan; relative stabilisation; Locarno 1925 | "Golden Twenties" in culture; fragile economy |
| 1929–1932 | Depression; ~6 million registered unemployed (early 1932) | Radicalisation |
| 1930 | Grand coalition collapses; Brüning governs by emergency decree | Presidential cabinets replace parliamentary government |
| 20 Jul 1932 | *Preußenschlag* | Democratic Prussian government deposed |
| Jul 1932 | NSDAP ~37% — largest party | |
| 30 Jan 1933 | Hitler appointed chancellor by Hindenburg | Not elected into power; installed by elites |

## H. Nazi dictatorship (1933–1945)

| Anchor | Event | What changed |
|---|---|---|
| 28 Feb 1933 | Reichstag Fire Decree | Basic rights suspended — never restored under Nazism |
| 23 Mar 1933 | Enabling Act (444 to 94; only SPD against) | Government legislates without parliament |
| 1933 | *Gleichschaltung*; unions dissolved (2 May); parties banned; April boycott; civil-service law | One-party state; exclusion of Jews from public service |
| 30 Jun 1934 | "Night of the Long Knives" | SA leadership murdered; army's acquiescence |
| Aug 1934 | Hitler combines chancellor & president; army oath | "Führer" state |
| 1935 | Nuremberg Laws | Jews stripped of citizenship; "race" in law |
| 1938 | Anschluss; Sudetenland; November pogrom | Expansion; open state-organised violence against Jews |
| 1 Sep 1939 | Invasion of Poland | WWII in Europe |
| 1939–1941 | "Euthanasia" murders (T4) | Mass murder of disabled people inside Germany |
| 22 Jun 1941 | Invasion of USSR | War of annihilation; Einsatzgruppen massacres |
| 20 Jan 1942 | Wannsee Conference | Coordination of genocide already under way |
| 1942–1944 | Death camps; forced labour at peak (≈13 m forced workers overall) | Holocaust: ~6 million Jews murdered |
| 20 Jul 1944 | Stauffenberg assassination attempt | Military-conservative resistance crushed |
| 8 May 1945 | Unconditional surrender | End of the German state as sovereign actor |

## I. Occupation and two German states (1945–1990)

| Anchor | Event | What changed |
|---|---|---|
| Jul–Aug 1945 | Potsdam Conference | Four zones; Oder–Neisse line; "orderly" transfer of Germans |
| 1945–1950 | Flight and expulsion of ~12 million Germans | Massive social mixing; East Prussia, Silesia, Pomerania lost |
| 1945–1946 | Nuremberg trials; denazification; SBZ land reform; SED forced merger (Apr 1946) | Divergent purges and social revolutions |
| Jun 1948 | Currency reforms; Berlin Blockade (to May 1949) | Division becomes economic reality |
| May 1949 | Basic Law; FRG founded | Parliamentary democracy designed against Weimar's failure |
| Oct 1949 | GDR founded | SED dictatorship under Soviet protection |
| 17 Jun 1953 | Uprising in GDR | Crushed by Soviet tanks |
| 1955 | FRG joins NATO; GDR Warsaw Pact; first guest-worker agreement (Italy) | Bloc integration; labour migration begins |
| 1957 | Treaties of Rome | FRG a founder of the EEC |
| 13 Aug 1961 | Berlin Wall | GDR stabilises by closing the exit |
| 1961 | Recruitment agreement with Turkey | |
| 1963–1965 | Frankfurt Auschwitz trial | West German public confronts perpetrators |
| 1968 | Student movement | Generational revolt; liberalisation |
| 1969–1973 | Brandt's *Ostpolitik*; Basic Treaty 1972 | Détente; mutual recognition in practice |
| 1971 | Honecker replaces Ulbricht | "Unity of economic and social policy" — consumer-welfare promises |
| 1973 | Recruitment stop | Guest workers settle; families join |
| 1975 | Helsinki Final Act | Human-rights language GDR dissidents could cite |
| 1976 | Biermann expatriation | Rupture with GDR intellectuals |

## J. United Germany (1990–today)

| Anchor | Event | What changed |
|---|---|---|
| 1990–1994 | Treuhand privatises ~8,500 firms | East German deindustrialisation; mass unemployment |
| 1991 | Bundestag votes to move capital to Berlin | |
| 1992–1993 | Arson attacks (Hoyerswerda, Rostock-Lichtenhagen, Mölln, Solingen); asylum compromise | Racist violence; restriction of asylum right |
