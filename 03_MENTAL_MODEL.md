## 1. The four "engines" of change

```
   WAR & GEOPOLITICS ──────┐
                           │
   ECONOMY & DEMOGRAPHY ───┼──►  CRISIS  ──►  NEW INSTITUTIONS  ──►  NEW EVERYDAY LIFE
                           │        ▲
   IDEAS & RELIGION ───────┤        │
                           │   ELITE CHOICES
   INSTITUTIONS & LAW ─────┘   (decisive in the short run)
```

- **War and geopolitics** — Germany's central location meant that wars (1618, 1806, 1866, 1918, 1945) repeatedly broke and remade its political order.
- **Economy and demography** — population pressure (1840s), industrial class formation (1850–1914), inflation (1923), depression (1929–33), catch-up growth (1950s), transformation shock (1990s).
- **Ideas and religion** — confession (1517 onwards), nationalism (1800 onwards), socialism, racism, democracy.
- **Institutions and law** — federalism, courts, bureaucracy, the army's constitutional position.
- **Elite choices** — the short-run hinge: 1866 (liberals accept Bismarck), 1914 (blank cheque), 1918 (Ebert–Groener pact), 1930–33 (presidential regime, appointment of Hitler), 1989 (security forces don't shoot).

## 2. Seven relationships to remember

1. **Fragmentation ↔ federal law.** German political fragmentation was not simply weakness; it came with law, courts and negotiation. Modern federalism is its heir.
2. **Religion ↔ territory ↔ politics.** Since 1555 confession tracked territory; for 400 years it predicted political behaviour (including the Nazi vote).
3. **Army autonomy ↔ catastrophe.** When the army was outside parliamentary control (1860s–1933), it could make war (1914), shift blame (1918), and help destroy democracy (1932–33). The FRG reversed this.
4. **Economic shock ↔ political radicalisation — mediated by elites.** Crises produce radical votes; whether they destroy democracy depends on what elites do (compare 1929–33 with the 1970s oil crises or 2008).
5. **Nationalism's direction flips.** Liberal and oppositional before 1848 → state-centred and exclusionary after 1871 → racial and genocidal under Nazism → civic and European after 1945 → contested again today.
6. **Reform from above.** German modernisation was often driven by bureaucracies rather than revolutions (1807, 1880s social insurance, 1948 currency reform) — producing effective states with limited participation (until 1949).
7. **Memory as institution.** Since the 1960s, remembrance of Nazi crimes has become part of the state's identity and its foreign policy.

## 3. The regime cycle

```
Crisis ─► Emergency powers / exceptional measures ─► Either:
   (a) Elites defend constitutional order → survival (1920, 1923, 1970s terrorism)
   (b) Elites use emergency powers against the constitution → dictatorship (1930–33)
   (c) External guarantor withdraws → collapse (1918 army; 1989 USSR)
```

## 4. Five questions to ask of any period

1. **Who controls the army, the police and the emergency powers?**
2. **Who decides who governs — voters, princes, a president, a party, a foreign power?**
3. **Which groups are excluded — and how?**
4. **What do the formal rules say, and what happens in practice?**
5. **What do ordinary people *do* — comply, profit, withdraw, protest, leave?**

## 5. Concept distinctions to keep straight

| Keep apart | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| German-speaking lands / German states / German nation / German Empire / Federal Republic | Different things, different eras |
| Holy Roman Empire / German Empire (Kaiserreich) / "Third Reich" | "Reich" meant very different polities |
| Prussia / Germany | Prussia was one state (partly non-German) — dominant 1866–1918, abolished 1947 |
| Constitution / power in practice | Almost every regime diverged |
| Propaganda / public opinion | Especially 1914, 1933–45, GDR |
| Resistance / non-conformity (*Resistenz*) / dissent | Different risk and effect |
| Nazi / GDR dictatorship | Comparable structures, incomparable crimes |

## 6. One-sentence version

Germany was for a thousand years a law-bound federation of many rulers; it was unified late by Prussian arms with democracy only half-built, broke catastrophically when its elites abandoned its first democracy in crisis, was divided and rebuilt by others, and has since made dispersed, constitutional, European democracy its answer to its own history — an answer now being tested.
