## (1) Carolingian kingship (c. 751–911)

**POWER** — Dynastic king anointed by Church → **INSTITUTIONS** — itinerant court, counts, *missi*, capitularies, assemblies → **ELITES** — imperial aristocracy, bishops, abbots → **STATE** — thin, personal, church-supported → **SOCIETY** — free/unfree; manorial estates → **ORDINARY PEOPLE** — lord, priest, tithe, military obligation.
**Constitution vs reality:** Capitularies prescribe order; enforcement depended on local magnates.

## (2) Medieval Holy Roman Empire (919–c.1500)

**POWER** — Elected king/emperor → **INSTITUTIONS** — electors (Golden Bull 1356), assemblies, feudal bonds, imperial church → **ELITES** — princes, prince-bishops, later cities → **STATE** — layered lordships; no central administration → **SOCIETY** — estates; towns as self-governing islands → **ORDINARY PEOPLE** — lordship (land, courts, persons), parish.
**Constitution vs reality:** No single constitution; ritual and privilege; emperor strong only through dynastic lands.

## (3) Early modern Empire + territories (1495–1806)

| Q | Answer |
|---|---|
| 1 | Emperor + Diet (three colleges); territorial princes |
| 2 | Princes in their territories; emperor via Habsburg power; Diet as congress |
| 3 | Election (emperor); inheritance (princes) |
| 4 | Law, courts, circles, confessional parity; standing armies (large territories) |
| 5 | Estates, courts, great powers |
| 6 | Territorial taxes granted by estates; domains; Reich: Roman months (military levies) |
| 7 | Territorial officials; imperial courts; circles |
| 8 | Estates (nobles, towns; rarely peasants) |
| 9 | Corporate privileges; right to sue; religious rights (1648) |
| 10 | Prison, exile |
| 11 | Parishes, schools, police ordinances |
| 12 | "More state": taxes, recruiters, pastors, courts |

**Chain:** POWER (princes) → INSTITUTIONS (Diet, courts, circles, territorial councils) → ELITES (nobles, officials, clergy) → STATE (territorial bureaucracies) → SOCIETY (estates, confessions) → ORDINARY PEOPLE (subjects of a prince; potential litigants in imperial courts).
**Constitution vs reality:** "Absolutist" claims vs negotiated rule; "sovereignty" myth vs Landeshoheit within imperial law.

## (4) Prussia (18th c.)

**Chain:** POWER (king) → INSTITUTIONS (General Directory, army, canton system) → ELITES (Junkers as officers and landlords) → STATE (fiscal-military bureaucracy) → SOCIETY (estates; Gutsherrschaft in the east) → ORDINARY PEOPLE (hereditary subjects of a Junker; soldiers).
**Constitution vs reality:** Absolute monarchy at the top; noble self-rule at the bottom.

## (5) Rhine Confederation states (1806–1813)

**Chain:** POWER (Napoleon + sovereign kings) → INSTITUTIONS (centralised ministries, gendarmerie, Code-inspired law) → ELITES (reforming bureaucrats) → STATE (uniform administration) → SOCIETY (legal equality in principle; religious equality) → ORDINARY PEOPLE (new rulers, conscription, end of serfdom in law).
**Constitution vs reality:** Constitutions promised representation; bureaucratic absolutism in practice.

## (6) German Confederation (1815–1866)

**Chain:** POWER (sovereign princes; Austria & Prussia) → INSTITUTIONS (Federal Assembly; Carlsbad system) → ELITES (princes, nobles, officials) → STATE (each member state) → SOCIETY (bourgeois associations; pauperism) → ORDINARY PEOPLE (subjects of Bavaria, Prussia…; censorship).
**Constitution vs reality:** Promise of representative constitutions (Art. 13) kept only in the south; league functioned as counter-revolutionary alliance.

## (7) Kaiserreich (1871–1918)

**Chain:** POWER (Kaiser/Prussia) → INSTITUTIONS (Bundesrat, Chancellor, army outside parliament, Reichstag) → ELITES (nobility, bureaucracy, industry, agrarians) → STATE (federal Rechtsstaat, social insurance) → SOCIETY (milieus: Protestant-conservative, liberal, Catholic, socialist) → ORDINARY PEOPLE (voters, conscripts, insured workers).
**Constitution vs reality:** Sovereignty "in the Bundesrat"; in fact Prussian hegemony, Kaiser's command power, and a Reichstag that could block but not govern — "system of avoided decisions."

## (8) Weimar Republic (1918/19–1933)

**Chain:** POWER (people → Reichstag; President) → INSTITUTIONS (coalitions; Art. 48) → ELITES (hostile army, judiciary, landowners; republican SPD/Centre/DDP) → STATE (parliamentary until 1930, presidential 1930–33) → SOCIETY (polarised milieus; paramilitaries) → ORDINARY PEOPLE (rights, welfare; inflation, unemployment, street violence).
**Constitution vs reality:** Parliamentary democracy on paper; from 1930 government by presidential decree and camarilla.

## (9) Nazi Germany (1933–1945)

**Chain:** POWER (Hitler's charismatic authority) → INSTITUTIONS (party, SS-police, ministries, Four-Year Plan, army — polycracy) → ELITES (Nazi leaders + cooperating traditional elites) → STATE ("Dual State": normative + prerogative) → SOCIETY (racial *Volksgemeinschaft*; mass organisations) → ORDINARY PEOPLE (jobs, leisure, fear, denunciation; exclusion and murder of "outsiders").
**Constitution vs reality:** Weimar constitution formally intact; overridden by emergency decree, Enabling Act and the Führer principle.

## (10) Allied occupation (1945–1949)

**Chain:** POWER (four Allies) → INSTITUTIONS (Control Council; zonal governments) → ELITES (Allied officers; licensed German politicians) → STATE (re-founded municipalities and Länder) → SOCIETY (refugees, ruins, black markets) → ORDINARY PEOPLE (questionnaires, tribunals, hunger).
**Constitution vs reality:** Potsdam's single economic unit vs rapid zonal division.

## (11) Federal Republic — Bonn (1949–1990)

**Chain:** POWER (voters → Bundestag → Chancellor) → INSTITUTIONS (Bundesrat, Constitutional Court, Bundesbank, social partners) → ELITES (party politicians, business, unions, churches; early Nazi-era continuities) → STATE (federal Rechtsstaat and welfare state) → SOCIETY (middle-class consumer society; guest workers) → ORDINARY PEOPLE (rights, prosperity, security).
**Constitution vs reality:** "Provisional" Basic Law became permanent; power more dispersed than the text alone suggests (courts, Länder, corporatism).

## (12) GDR (1949–1990)

**Chain:** POWER (SED Politburo; USSR) → INSTITUTIONS (nomenklatura, Stasi, bloc parties, mass organisations) → ELITES (party apparatus; new intelligentsia) → STATE (planned economy; closed border) → SOCIETY (workers' state in name; niches) → ORDINARY PEOPLE (security, shortages, surveillance, confinement).
**Constitution vs reality:** Democratic and later socialist constitutional forms; real power in the party leadership; Volkskammer unanimous.

## (13) United Germany — Berlin Republic (1990–)

**Chain:** POWER (voters; coalitions) → INSTITUTIONS (Basic Law system + EU) → ELITES (predominantly West German-origin; increasingly diverse) → STATE (federal, EU-embedded, welfare state) → SOCIETY (diverse, ageing, regionally unequal) → ORDINARY PEOPLE (rights, social security; East–West and migration debates; rising distrust).
**Constitution vs reality:** Close alignment; tensions around EU competences, militant democracy (AfD), and coalition-building in a fragmented system.
