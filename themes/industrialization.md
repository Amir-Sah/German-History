## 1. Economic systems over time

| Era | System | Who owned what | Who controlled labour | State role |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Medieval | Manorial agrarian economy + towns | Lords (lay/church) owned land; peasants held tenures | Lordship; guilds in towns | Tolls, mints, market privileges |
| Early modern | Grund-/Gutsherrschaft; cameralism; guilds | East: noble estates; West: peasant tenures | Serfdom (east); guild regulation | Revenue maximisation; manufactures |
| 1800–1850 | Liberalisation (serfdom abolished; freedom of trade); Zollverein | Private property in land; emerging capital | Market wages; pauperism | Reforms from above; customs union |
| 1850–1914 | Industrial capitalism; "organised capitalism" | Industrialists, banks; cartels | Factory discipline; unions | Tariffs, social insurance, railways (often state-owned) |
| 1914–1918 | War economy | Private, under military direction | Auxiliary Service Law | Planning, borrowing |
| 1918/19–1933 | Capitalism + welfare state; inflation; depression | Private; big concerns (IG Farben 1925, Vereinigte Stahlwerke 1926) | Collective bargaining; unemployment insurance | Arbitration; reparations; deflation |
| 1933–1945 | Private capitalism under state direction; autarky; plunder | Private ("Aryan"); expropriated Jewish property | DAF; wage freeze; forced labour | Four-Year Plan; rearmament |
| FRG | Social market economy | Private; co-determination | Tarifautonomie; works councils | Rules, welfare, Bundesbank |
| GDR | Planned economy | State (VEB); collectives (LPG) | State unions; plan norms | Total planning |
| Since 1990 | Social market economy in EU; globalisation | Private; Treuhand privatised the East | Declining bargaining coverage; minimum wage | EU rules; debt brake; 2025 investment fund |

## 2. Economic change → political change (key links)

| Economic change | Political consequence |
|---|---|
| Population growth + pauperism (1830s–40s) | Pressure behind 1848 |
| Zollverein (1834) | Prussian economic leadership (not destiny) |
| 1873 crash and "Great Depression" | Protectionism, anti-liberal turn, organised antisemitism |
| Industrial working class | SPD's rise; social insurance |
| War financing by debt (1914–18) | Inflation 1923 → middle-class alienation |
| Depression + deflation (1929–32) | Nazi breakthrough |
| Rearmament & plunder (1933–45) | Full employment, war, genocide-linked robbery |
| Currency reform + Marshall Plan + catch-up (1948–) | Legitimacy of West German democracy |
| GDR stagnation & debt (1970s–89) | Collapse of the SED state |
| Currency union + Treuhand (1990) | East German deindustrialisation and lasting grievances |
| Energy dependence on Russia (–2022) | Zeitenwende shock |

### If you remember only 5 things

1. Germany moved from lordship-based agriculture to Europe's leading industrial economy in barely a century.
2. Economic shocks — 1873, 1923, 1929–32, 1990 — repeatedly reshaped politics.
3. German capitalism has usually been "organised": banks, cartels, associations, co-determination.
4. The welfare state began as a tool against socialism and became a pillar of democracy.
5. The GDR's planned economy failed; its end brought a transformation shock still felt today.
