## 1. Shape of the story

German nationalism changed its **carriers**, its **enemies** and its **political direction** several times:

| Phase | Carriers | Main enemy | Political direction |
|---|---|---|---|
| c. 1500 humanism | Scholars (Celtis, Hutten) | Rome/papacy, Italians' contempt | Cultural-imperial pride |
| 1770–1806 | Writers, philosophers | French cultural dominance | Cultural nation |
| 1806–1815 | Intellectuals, students, Prussian reformers | Napoleon/France | Anti-French; partly ethnic (Arndt, Jahn, Fichte) |
| 1815–1848 | Liberal bourgeoisie, students, gymnasts, singers | Princes' particularism; Metternich | **Liberal-oppositional**: unity *with* freedom |
| 1848–49 | National Assembly | Princes; also Danes, Poles | Liberal-democratic but exclusionary at borders |
| 1860s | National Association; Prussian state | Austria; France | Unity *before* freedom; Realpolitik |
| 1871–1914 | State, schools, army, veterans, pressure groups (Pan-Germans, Navy League) | Internal "enemies of the Reich" (Catholics, socialists, Poles, Jews); Britain | **Conservative, state-centred, imperialist, increasingly völkisch** |
| 1914–1918 | Entire establishment; "Ideas of 1914" | Entente | War nationalism; annexationism |
| 1918–1933 | Right-wing parties, veterans' leagues, youth, universities | Versailles; the republic; "November criminals"; Jews; Poles | **Revisionist, anti-republican, völkisch** |
| 1933–1945 | Nazi state | Jews ("world enemy"), Bolshevism, Slavs | **Racial, genocidal, expansionist** |
| 1945–1990 | West: muted; expellee associations; GDR: socialist patriotism | West: communism; GDR: "imperialism" | Suppressed; replaced by constitutional patriotism / socialist nation |
| 1990– | Mainstream: football patriotism (2006 World Cup), constitutional patriotism; radical right: AfD, neo-Nazis | Migrants, Islam, EU, "globalists" (radical right) | Mostly civic; radical-right ethnic nationalism rising |

## 2. Key analytical points

- **Liberal origins:** Before 1848, nationalism was primarily an opposition movement for constitutional freedom. The *switch* to conservative, state-centred nationalism happened after 1866–71, when the state fulfilled the national goal.
- **Negative integration:** The Kaiserreich defined the nation partly *against* internal groups — a key mechanism of exclusion.
- **Radical nationalism from below:** Geoff Eley showed that pressure groups pushed the government toward more aggressive nationalism — not only elite manipulation.
- **Defeat radicalised nationalism:** 1918 turned it revisionist and racial; the stab-in-the-back myth fused nationalism with antisemitism and anti-Marxism.
- **Post-1945 taboo and return:** West German national symbols were used cautiously; the 2006 World Cup is often cited as normalising flag display.

## 3. Debates

- Civic vs ethnic (Kohn) — too schematic.
- Continuity from 19th-century völkisch nationalism to Nazism (Mosse) vs discontinuities.
- Whether the radical right today represents continuity or a new European populist phenomenon.

### If you remember only 5 things

1. German nationalism was born liberal and oppositional.
2. After 1871 it became conservative, state-centred and exclusionary.
3. Defeat in 1918 made it revisionist, racial and antisemitic — the soil for Nazism.
4. After 1945 it was largely replaced by constitutional and European identities.
5. An ethnic nationalism has re-emerged on the radical right since the 2010s.
