## 1. Overview

Women's legal, economic and political position changed through distinct regimes: medieval guardianship with real economic roles; the early modern "whole house" (*das ganze Haus*) with husband as head; the 19th-century bourgeois ideal of separate spheres, codified in the Civil Code (1900); suffrage and formal equality in Weimar (1918–19); Nazi motherhood ideology combined with exclusion and mobilisation; post-1945 divergence — West: legal equality in the constitution but the "housewife marriage" in civil law until 1977; East: near-universal female employment and childcare, but a double burden and few women at the top; since 1990: convergence toward the dual-earner model, with East–West differences persisting.

## 3. Debates

- Were women "victims" or also "perpetrators" under Nazism (Claudia Koonz vs Gisela Bock debate, 1980s)? Consensus: both, depending on position — women as supporters, beneficiaries, perpetrators, and victims.
- Was the GDR emancipatory? Real gains in employment and reproductive rights; persistent patriarchal structures.

### If you remember only 5 things

1. Women were economic actors in every era, but legally subordinated for most of history.
2. Suffrage came with revolution in 1918; the Civil Code's patriarchal marriage law lasted until 1977 in the West.
3. Nazism glorified motherhood while persecuting "undesirable" women and relying on women's support.
4. The GDR pushed women into employment and provided childcare; the FRG long preferred the housewife model.
5. East–West differences in women's work and pay persist today.
