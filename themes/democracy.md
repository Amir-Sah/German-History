## 1. Five democratic moments and their fate

| Moment | What was achieved | What limited or ended it | Legacy |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Communal and corporate self-government** (medieval towns, village communities, estates) | Councils, charters, courts, negotiated rule | Oligarchy; not individual rights; princely absolutism | A tradition of law-bound, negotiated authority (Blickle's "communalism") |
| **1848/49** | National parliament, universal male suffrage, basic rights, constitution | Princes' armies; divisions; national conflicts | Paulskirche constitution as "foundational document of German democracy" (Osterhammel) |
| **Kaiserreich electoral democracy (1871–1918)** | Universal, equal, secret male suffrage; mass parties; high turnout | No parliamentary government; army autonomy; Prussian three-class vote | Democratic practice in elections (Anderson); unresolved power question |
| **Weimar (1918/19–33)** | Full parliamentary democracy; women's suffrage; social rights | Elite disloyalty; Art. 48; economic crisis; anti-system parties; appointment of Hitler | Negative lessons embedded in the Basic Law |
| **Federal Republic (1949–)** | Stable parliamentary democracy with constitutional court, federalism, militant democracy | Early authoritarian continuities; now fragmentation and radical-right challenge | The longest-lasting German democracy — 77 years (West) and 36 as united Germany |

Plus **1989 in the East**: a Peaceful Revolution that created democracy from below, then merged into the FRG.

## 2. Why democracy failed (1933) and succeeded (after 1949) — comparison

| Factor | Weimar | Bonn |
|---|---|---|
| Elite attitudes | Army, judiciary, bureaucracy, landowners hostile | Old elites discredited, dependent on Allies; new political class |
| Economy | Inflation, depression | Economic miracle |
| International setting | Versailles resentment; no security guarantee | Western integration, NATO, Marshall Plan, European integration |
| Constitution | Strong president, Art. 48, negative no-confidence, no threshold | Weak president, constructive no-confidence, 5% threshold, Constitutional Court, militant democracy |
| Party system | Fragmented, milieu-based, anti-system parties strong | Concentrated catch-all parties; anti-system parties banned or weak |
| Legitimacy | Associated with defeat and humiliation | Associated with prosperity and Western belonging |
| Political culture | Anti-democratic intellectual climate | Gradual democratic learning (1960s turning point) |

## 3. Debates

- **Was the Kaiserreich democratising?** Anderson, Richter (yes, substantially) vs Wehler, Conze, Herbert (authoritarian structures dominant).
- **Weimar: doomed or destroyed?** Recent scholarship stresses contingency and elite choice.
- **FRG: success story or incomplete?** Wolfrum's "successful democracy" vs critiques of continuities and exclusion.
- **Present:** Are the Basic Law's safeguards sufficient against the radical right? (AfD classification; Brandmauer.)

### If you remember only 5 things

1. Germany has a long democratic tradition — 1848, universal male suffrage from 1867/71, Weimar — not only authoritarian ones.
2. Democracy failed in 1933 because elites abandoned it in an economic catastrophe, not because Germans were incapable of it.
3. The Bonn Republic succeeded thanks to discredited old elites, prosperity, Western integration and an anti-Weimar constitution.
4. East Germans made their own democratic revolution in 1989.
5. Today's challenge is whether dispersed, consensual democracy can absorb fragmentation and radical-right strength.
