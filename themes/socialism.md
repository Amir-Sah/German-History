## 1. The German labour movement's arc

**Origins (1840s):** Artisans' associations, the Communist League (Marx & Engels, *Manifesto* 1848), Stephan Born's Arbeiterverbrüderung (1848).
**Founding (1863–1875):** Lassalle's ADAV (1863) + Bebel/Liebknecht's SDAP (1869) → Gotha merger (1875).
**Persecution and growth (1878–1890):** Anti-Socialist Law; party grows anyway.
**Mass party (1890–1914):** Erfurt Programme (1891) — Marxist theory, reformist practice; revisionism debate (Bernstein); largest party (1912); ~1 million members; ~2.5 million union members.
**War and split (1914–1920):** War credits (1914); USPD split (1917); Spartacus League → KPD (Dec 1918/Jan 1919).
**Weimar (1918/19–33):** SPD as republic's main pillar; KPD as mass revolutionary party aligned with Moscow; bitter enmity (Freikorps murders of Luxemburg and Liebknecht, 1919; KPD's "social fascism" line from 1928).
**Destruction (1933):** Unions dissolved (2 May), SPD banned (June), KPD crushed (spring); exile and underground resistance.
**Division (1945–1990):**
- *West:* SPD re-founded under Kurt Schumacher (anti-communist); Godesberg Programme (1959) abandons Marxism → governing party (1966, 1969–82).
- *East:* Forced merger of KPD and SPD into SED (April 1946); Social Democrats purged ("Social Democratism" as an offence); "real existing socialism."
**After 1990:** SED → PDS → Die Linke (2007 merger with WASG); SPD's decline after Agenda 2010; BSW split (2024); Die Linke's revival (2025 federal; first in Berlin 2026).

## 2. Three German socialisms compared

| | Social democracy (SPD) | Communism (KPD/SED) | "Real socialism" in power (GDR) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Goal | Democratic socialism → social market economy (after 1959) | Proletarian revolution, dictatorship of the proletariat | Party-state socialism |
| Method | Elections, unions, reforms | Revolution; Leninist party | Planning, nationalisation, repression |
| Relation to democracy | Pillar of Weimar and FRG | Hostile to "bourgeois" democracy | Dictatorship |
| Achievements | 8-hour day, works councils, welfare state, co-determination, Ostpolitik | Resistance to Nazism (at great cost); in GDR: social security, women's work | Social equality in some respects; failed economy |
| Failures | 1914 war credits; 1918–19 reliance on Freikorps; tolerated Brüning | Attacks on SPD in Weimar; Stalinism | Repression, Wall, economic collapse |

## 3. Debates

- **The SPD in 1918–19:** Did Ebert's pact with the army and the use of Freikorps "betray" the revolution (left critique; Sebastian Haffner) or save democracy from Bolshevism (Heinrich August Winkler)? **CONTESTED.**
- **Was the SPD "revolutionary" before 1914?** Rhetoric vs practice (Roth, Nipperdey).
- **Left division and 1933:** How much did the SPD–KPD split contribute to Nazi victory? Significant, but in the last free elections (July and November 1932) SPD + KPD together (~36–37%) still trailed NSDAP + DNVP (~42–43%), so unity alone would not have produced a majority.

### If you remember only 5 things

1. Germany produced the world's model socialist party — the SPD — under persecution in the 1880s.
2. Its revolutionary rhetoric hid reformist practice; that made it the backbone of Weimar and later the FRG.
3. The split with communism (1914–19) never healed and weakened the left against Nazism.
4. In the East, communism ruled for 40 years by absorbing and purging social democracy.
5. The SPD and the Left's heirs remain key actors today — social democracy's welfare state is a defining German institution.
