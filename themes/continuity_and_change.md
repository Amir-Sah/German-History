## 1. How modern Germany inherited institutions from earlier periods

| Institution today | Origin | Path |
|---|---|---|
| **Federalism (16 Länder, Bundesrat)** | Territorial principalities of the Holy Roman Empire | Confederation (1815) → Bundesrat of the Kaiserreich (1871) → Weimar Reichsrat → broken by Nazis → restored by Allies and Basic Law (1949) |
| **Bundesrat composed of state governments** | Imperial Diet as congress of princes' envoys; Confederation's Federal Assembly | Kaiserreich Bundesrat (1871) → Basic Law |
| **Constitutional review / judicial checks on rulers** | Imperial courts (Reichskammergericht, Reichshofrat) where subjects could sue princes | 19th-c. Rechtsstaat → Weimar Staatsgerichtshof → Federal Constitutional Court (1951) |
| **Professional civil service (*Berufsbeamtentum*)** | Cameralist officials; Prussian reforms | Kaiserreich → Weimar → Nazi purge/service → Art. 33(5) GG "traditional principles" |
| **Social insurance** | Bismarck (1883–89) | Weimar unemployment insurance (1927) → FRG → care insurance (1995) |
| **Church tax and church welfare** | State-church ties since the Reformation; Weimar church articles (1919) | Incorporated into Basic Law (Art. 140) |
| **Co-determination & collective bargaining** | 1918 Stinnes–Legien agreement; Weimar works councils (1920) | FRG laws 1951/1952/1976 |
| **Dual vocational training** | Guild apprenticeship | Craft code (1897) → Vocational Training Act (1969) |
| **Local self-government** | Medieval towns; Prussian Municipal Ordinance (1808) | Art. 28 GG |
| **Paulskirche basic rights** | 1848/49 | Weimar Part II (1919) → Basic Law Arts. 1–19 |
| **Black-red-gold** | 1815 student corps; 1832 Hambach; 1848 | Weimar flag → FRG and GDR (GDR with emblem from 1959) |
| **National anthem text** | Hoffmann von Fallersleben, 1841 | Third stanza only (since 1952/1991) |
| **Memory culture** | Post-1945, especially 1960s–80s | Memorials, education, "Staatsräson" |

## 2. Fundamental breaks

| Break | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| 1517–1555 | One church | Territorial confessions |
| 1803–1806 | Empire, church states, imperial cities | Sovereign middle states |
| 1807–1850 | Serfdom, estates | Legal equality, property markets |
| 1871 | Many states | Nation-state (kleindeutsch) |
| 1918–19 | Monarchy | Republic, universal suffrage |
| 1933 | Democracy | Dictatorship |
| 1945 | Nation-state, eastern territories, Junkers | Occupation, lost East, expulsion, division |
| 1949 East / 1952 | Private property, Länder | State socialism, districts |
| 1989–90 | Division, SED dictatorship | Unity, democracy for the East |

## 3. Continuities across regime changes (often underestimated)

- **Personnel:** 1918→Weimar (officers, judges, officials); 1933→Nazi (most civil servants stayed); 1945→FRG (most judges, many officials, doctors, professors); GDR: fewer continuities, but also former Nazis in some roles.
- **Regional identities** outlasting states (Bavaria, Saxony).
- **Confessional geography** from 1555/1648 visible in voting until the 1960s (and Nazi voting in 1932).
- **East–West agrarian divergence** (Gutsherrschaft vs Grundherrschaft) shaping 19th–20th-c. social structures in the East, then erased by 1945 land reform.
- **Export-oriented, organised capitalism** from the Kaiserreich to today.

## 4. The key analytical warning

Continuities are real, but **continuity ≠ causation**. The fact that federalism, the civil service or nationalism existed before 1933 does not mean they caused 1933; the same institutions underpinned the successful Federal Republic. What mattered was *how* they were combined, *who* controlled them, and *what* choices were made in crises.

### If you remember only 5 things

1. Modern German federalism descends from the Holy Roman Empire's territorial princes.
2. Social insurance, co-determination and the civil service are 19th–early-20th-century inventions that survived every regime.
3. The Basic Law's rights descend from 1848 and 1919.
4. Personnel continuity across regime changes was enormous — especially 1918, 1933 and 1945.
5. Continuity is not causation: the same institutions served both catastrophe and success.
