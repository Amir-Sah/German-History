## 1. Overview

German education has been shaped by the Church (medieval), the Reformation (catechism, schools for literacy), territorial states (compulsory schooling decrees from the 17th–18th centuries), neohumanism (Humboldt's Gymnasium and research university, 1809–10), the Kaiserreich's tiered and nationalist schooling, Weimar's reforms (common primary school, 1920), Nazi ideological control, and post-1945 divergence: federal, tracked schooling in the West; unified polytechnic schooling in the East. Universities have been central to German identity and national movements (and to Nazi complicity).

## 2. Timeline table

| Era | Key features | Who got educated |
|---|---|---|
| Medieval | Cathedral and monastery schools; universities from 1348 (Prague), 1365 (Vienna), 1386 (Heidelberg), 1388 (Cologne) | Clergy, elites; some urban Latin/German schools |
| Reformation | Luther: every child should read scripture; Protestant schools; Jesuit colleges (Catholic) | Broader literacy in Protestant towns |
| 17th–18th c. | Compulsory schooling decrees (Weimar 1619; Prussia 1717/1763) — weakly enforced | Rising literacy, uneven by region |
| 1800–1850 | Humboldt: Gymnasium (classical), research university (Berlin 1810); Abitur | *Bildungsbürgertum*; state officials |
| Kaiserreich | Near-universal elementary literacy; tiered system (Volksschule/Realschule/Gymnasium); technical universities; nationalist curricula; women admitted to universities 1900–1908 | Masses (elementary), elites (Gymnasium); world-leading science |
| Weimar | Common four-year primary school (*Grundschule*, 1920); expansion | Slight broadening |
| Nazi | Purges of Jewish and dissident teachers/professors (~15% of university teachers dismissed 1933–38 — estimate); racial curricula; Hitler Youth rivals school | Jewish children excluded 1938 |
| FRG | Länder control ("cultural sovereignty"); early tracking at 10; expansion 1960s–70s (new universities); vocational dual system | Mass higher education by 2000s; strong link to parental background (PISA 2001 shock) |
| GDR | Ten-year polytechnic school (1959); politicised; access to *Erweiterte Oberschule* and universities tied to class origin and political loyalty | High basic education; restricted elite access for dissenters/church families |
| Since 1990 | East adopts western structures (with variations); Bologna reform; *Exzellenzinitiative* | Debates on inequality, digitalisation, teacher shortages |

## 3. Why education matters for the "operating system"

- **Bildung** gave the educated middle class status independent of birth — and tied it to state service (officials, teachers, professors as civil servants).
- **Universities** were crucibles of nationalism (Burschenschaften), of antisemitism (Treitschke), of Nazi student enthusiasm (Nazi student league won university elections before 1933), and later of 1968.
- **Vocational training** (dual system) links industry, guild traditions and the social partners.

### If you remember only 5 things

1. The Reformation and territorial states pushed literacy earlier than in many regions of Europe.
2. Humboldt's university and Gymnasium created a status group defined by education.
3. The tiered school system has long reproduced social class — still visible in PISA results.
4. Universities were centres of nationalism and, before 1933, of Nazi student enthusiasm.
5. East and West built different school systems; after 1990 the West's model spread east.
